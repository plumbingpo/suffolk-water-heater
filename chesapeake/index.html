Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Chesapeake

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Around Chesapeake, water heater trouble usually come from a handful of sources. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater on hard water needs regular descaling. And an older install getting a new heater often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early.
