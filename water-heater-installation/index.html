Every one of them involves new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Before any pipe is cut, we match the heater to your household. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. A heat pump model has to have breathing room, temperatures above roughly 40 degrees, and a condensate drain. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. If you notice any of these signs, contact Suffolk Water Heater at (757) 317-9870 for a prompt inspection.

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Professional installation gets every piece right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater, calculate the gas line, lay out the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and add the code items your location calls for. Leaks, draft, and carbon monoxide are tested, and the heater is left at a safe temperature with the paperwork handled. It is the gap between a heater that just works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts when the weather turns. And if a simpler option would serve you just as well, we tell you that too, without pressure.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here are our recommendations:

  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
