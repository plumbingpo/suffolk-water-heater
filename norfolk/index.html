Keeping Your Norfolk Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
