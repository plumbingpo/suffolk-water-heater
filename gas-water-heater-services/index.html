A gas water heater is a refreshingly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In Suffolk, VA, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Suffolk, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Keep one rule in mind: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Suffolk Water Heater can take over from there.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. We replace thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, and igniters, clean burners and flame arrestor screens, swap failed gas control valves, and correct vent connectors that have separated or lost their slope. We also flush sediment, the cause of that popping or rumbling sound and a major reason tanks overheat at the bottom and fail early. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations are sized by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and include the right venting, a sediment trap on the gas line, an expansion tank where needed, and seismic straps and a drain pan where code requires them. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, largely because you cannot see the biggest hazards. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. And many repairs, including gas valve and heater replacement, require a permit and inspection in most areas. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
