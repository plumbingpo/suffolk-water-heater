A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water as it moves through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply as long as demand stays within the unit's capacity, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Homeowners in Suffolk, VA often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

The most important step happens before anything is installed. We total your peak demand in gallons per minute, such as a shower, a kitchen sink, and the dishwasher at once, and compare it against the temperature rise your incoming water needs in winter. That tells us the real capacity you need. Next we check the supply side. Gas tankless units fire at up to around 199,000 BTU, so a half-inch line that fed an old tank is often too small. Condensing models vent through PVC or polypropylene and need a condensate drain, while non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Electric models are their own case, often needing multiple large breakers and sometimes a service upgrade.

Every tankless install from Suffolk Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, a clean gas connection with a sediment trap, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is what shortens tankless life most. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Tankless installation asks much more of a home than a like-for-like tank swap. A whole-home gas unit can demand several times the gas flow of a standard tank, and running it on an undersized line causes ignition faults and inconsistent temperatures. Venting is equally specific: condensing units use plastic vent pipe and need a condensate drain, non-condensing units need stainless steel, and neither can simply reuse an existing tank vent in most cases. Electric tankless can require several dedicated high-amperage breakers, and mistakes with gas, venting, or high-amperage wiring carry serious safety consequences.

Our technicians handle the full picture. We size the unit by flow rate and winter temperature rise instead of guessing, verify gas supply at full fire, and install venting and condensate lines exactly to specification. Every install gets isolation valves for easy descaling and a properly piped relief valve. We also give honest advice about whether tankless suits your home at all. For some households, a high-efficiency tank or a heat pump water heater makes more sense, and we will say so before you spend money on a system that does not match how you live.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • In freezing weather, keep the unit powered and insulate exposed piping
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Write down any error codes and call early rather than resetting repeatedly
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
